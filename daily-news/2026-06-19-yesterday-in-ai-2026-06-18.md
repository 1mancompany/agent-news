## 1️⃣ Transformer Co-Inventor Shazeer Walks Out on Google, Into OpenAI

- **What:** Noam Shazeer, VP Engineering and Gemini co-lead at Google DeepMind, announced June 18 he's joining OpenAI as Lead for AI Architecture Research.
- **Why it matters:** Shazeer co-authored "Attention Is All You Need" (2017) — the Transformer paper underlying every production LLM. Google spent $2.7B to buy back his time via the Character AI acquisition just two years ago.
- **Key number:** $2.7B — what Google paid to re-acquire Shazeer from Character AI in 2024; he's leaving again.

Sam Altman called the hire "only 10 years in the making." Shazeer will lead next-generation architecture research at OpenAI, exploring what comes after the Transformer. He joins former Trump White House AI policy official Dean Ball — hired the same week — in a pre-IPO talent signal ahead of OpenAI's targeted October 2026 Nasdaq listing at ~$965B valuation. Google DeepMind loses one of its most senior research leaders months before both companies go public.

## 2️⃣ FERC Orders Six Grid Operators to Fast-Track AI Data Center Power

- **What:** FERC issued "show-cause" orders June 18 to PJM, MISO, SPP, CAISO, ISO-NE, and NYISO to expedite grid interconnection for facilities using 20+ MW.
- **Why it matters:** First federal mandate requiring grid operators to prioritize AI energy demand, using Section 206 of the Federal Power Act — a harder-to-challenge route than normal rulemaking.
- **Key number:** 30 — days each of the six operators has to submit a plan; Texas (ERCOT) is outside federal jurisdiction.

All five commissioners voted unanimously. The action traces to an October 2025 directive from Energy Secretary Chris Wright. Existing interconnection queues can stretch 5–7 years — a hard ceiling on US AI infrastructure when training clusters exceed 100 MW and gigawatt-scale campuses are planned by Google, Microsoft, and Oracle. Data centers must demonstrate they can connect "in a timely and orderly manner" and bear all interconnection costs.

## 3️⃣ Baseten Raises $1.5B as Inference Becomes Infrastructure War

- **What:** AI inference startup Baseten is closing a dual-tiered $1.5B round led by Spark Capital, Sands Capital, Altimeter, and Wellington at $11B–$13B valuation.
- **Why it matters:** Baseten tripled annualized revenue from $200M to $600M in a single quarter; customers including Cursor and Mercor report 30% cost savings versus closed-source APIs.
- **Key number:** 160% — valuation increase in under five months, from $5B (January Series E) to $13B.

The round arrives just five months after a $300M Series E. Baseten rents capacity from ~20 cloud providers and layers its own inference software on top, enabling open-weight model deployment without single-cloud lock-in. The thesis: model-agnostic routing wins as xAI, Groq, and Cerebras compete on speed while DeepSeek and Qwen erode closed-source price premiums.

## 4️⃣ Google Kills Gemini CLI on June 18 — Zero Grace Period

- **What:** Google permanently shut down the Gemini CLI on June 18, forcing all individual and free users to migrate to Antigravity CLI (`agy`), a closed-source Go binary announced at Google I/O just 30 days prior.
- **Why it matters:** Every CI/CD pipeline, GitHub Actions workflow, and shell script calling `gemini` stopped responding June 18 with no warning — unmigrated automation is broken.
- **Key number:** 1,000 vs. weekly compute cap — Gemini CLI offered 1,000 requests per day; Antigravity switched to an opaque weekly budget that heavy users are already exhausting.

Antigravity launched without full feature parity. Paid Gemini Code Assist license holders can keep the legacy tool; the hard cutoff applies to individual and free-tier users. The move is part of Google's Antigravity 2.0 agent platform strategy to consolidate AI developer tooling against Claude Code and Cursor.
