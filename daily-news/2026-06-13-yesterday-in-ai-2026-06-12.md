## 1️⃣ US Export Order Shuts Down Fable 5 and Mythos 5 Three Days After Launch

- **What:** Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick sent Anthropic a letter at 5:21 PM ET on June 12 ordering immediate suspension of Claude Fable 5 and Mythos 5 under export control law, citing a claimed jailbreak by an unnamed company.
- **Why it matters:** It is the first time the US government has used export controls to force a commercial AI lab to pull a deployed frontier model offline worldwide — and Anthropic's own rebuttal says the triggering demonstration only exposed minor, previously known vulnerabilities.
- **Key number:** 3 — days from Fable 5's public launch (June 9) to its forced global shutdown.

Anthropic had no practical alternative: a nationality-based export restriction covering foreign nationals inside and outside the US is impossible to enforce selectively on a shared cloud service, so both models came down for every customer globally. All prior Anthropic models remain accessible.

An administration official who spoke to Axios framed the decision around national security — an unnamed company had demonstrated it could jailbreak Mythos, alarming the administration. Anthropic reviewed the demo and said it found only a small number of previously known, minor software vulnerabilities. Its public statement called the order a "misunderstanding" and said it was working to restore access, with no timetable given.

The move carries a sharp irony: Anthropic's own candid hazard disclosures about Mythos-class capability levels appear to have accelerated the government's concern. The company that warned loudest about its model's power was the first to have it switched off by Washington.

## 2️⃣ Meta's 6,500-Engineer AI Unit Erupts in Open Revolt

- **What:** A Wired investigation published June 12 revealed that Meta's Applied AI division — a three-month-old unit of roughly 6,500 engineers forced to generate AI training data — is near collapse, including a hijacked company livestream and Zuckerberg admitting in an internal memo that Meta "made mistakes."
- **Why it matters:** It is the most public AI labor revolt inside a hyperscaler yet, and the meltdown suggests that mass-reassigning engineers to AI data-labeling work as a cost-cutting and capability play carries serious organizational risk.
- **Key number:** 1,600+ — Meta employees who signed a petition protesting company-wide keylogging and click-tracking under its AI training data program.

Workers described being coerced into the Applied AI unit under a "join or quit" ultimatum, where their job is generating coding puzzles and exercises to train models — work many call pointless make-work. One current employee told Wired: "It's literally the gulag." The situation boiled over during an internal company stream when an employee interrupted proceedings to demand that a senior executive be told he was "a piece of s**t."

Zuckerberg responded the same day in an internal memo acknowledging the company had made mistakes and promising no further company-wide layoffs in 2026. Meta's chief product officer Chris Cox separately called the environment "brutal" on a call with staff. Meta had already scaled back the keystroke-monitoring program on June 2, allowing employees to pause data collection for up to 30 minutes.

## 3️⃣ SpaceX IPO Breaks Records — and Takes xAI Public With It

- **What:** SpaceX began trading on Nasdaq (SPCX) on June 12, priced at $135/share, opening at $150 (+11%) and closing at $161 (+19%), raising $75B in the largest IPO in Wall Street history — and because SpaceX acquired xAI in February 2026, Grok and xAI's compute infrastructure went public along with it.
- **Why it matters:** For AI observers, this is the first time xAI's models, data centers, and the X social network data moat have been embedded in a publicly-traded entity, making Elon Musk's AI business part of a $1.77T market cap.
- **Key number:** $75B — raised at IPO, more than double Saudi Aramco's 2019 record.

SpaceX's S-1 disclosed 9,600 Starlink satellites in orbit, 10.3 million subscribers, and $19.3B in revenue as of March 31. The filing confirmed that SpaceX's AI1 satellites — onboard compute nodes for future Starlink hardware — are targeting launch late next year, with some compute capabilities being added to existing Starlink satellites beforehand.

The xAI acquisition in February folded Grok, X (formerly Twitter), and xAI's GPU clusters into SpaceX's corporate structure. That means the IPO effectively valued xAI's AI capabilities and the X data moat as a publicly-priced asset for the first time, bundled into a $1.77T company whose stock surged past $2T within the first hour of trading.
