## 1️⃣ Claude Writes 80% of Anthropic's Own Code — Now Asking for a Global Pause Button

- **What:** Anthropic published "When AI Builds Itself," a report revealing Claude now authors more than 80% of the company's merged production code — up from single digits before Claude Code launched in February 2025
- **Why it matters:** Anthropic is the first major frontier lab to quantify recursive AI development at this scale and to publicly call for a verifiable, multilateral pause mechanism before AI systems can design their own successors
- **Key number:** 80% of production code authored by Claude; engineers now ship 8× more code per quarter than any point between 2021 and 2024

The report, authored by co-founder Jack Clark and Anthropic Institute director Marina Favaro, draws on public benchmarks and previously unreported internal data. Claude's success rate on open-ended coding tasks reached 76% in May — up 50 percentage points in just six months. The report distinguishes between "AI-accelerated development" and true recursive self-improvement, warning the threshold could arrive faster than governments are prepared for.

Anthropic stops short of claiming recursive self-improvement has begun. Instead it argues the inflection point is close enough to warrant pre-emptive infrastructure: a coordinated, verifiable mechanism all major labs could jointly trigger — analogous to existing treaty frameworks for nuclear and biological weapons.

On the same day, a multi-hour outage took down claude.ai, Claude API, Claude Code, and Claude Cowork — elevated error rates ran from 8:08 PT until full restoration at 18:27 UTC, the company's most significant platform disruption since its 2025 infrastructure expansion.

## 2️⃣ Apple Approves First AI Agent Inside iMessage — Poke Gets a Billion-User Channel

- **What:** Apple approved Poke as the first standalone AI agent to operate inside Messages for Business, the iMessage channel that companies use to reach customers directly in Apple's native Messages app
- **Why it matters:** For roughly one billion iMessage users, this is the first time a third-party AI agent appears directly inside Apple's Messages — a distribution channel Apple has tightly gatekept for years
- **Key number:** 100 million messages already relayed by Poke before gaining iMessage access

Poke launched in March 2026 as a text-first AI agent for everyday users, accessible over SMS, Telegram, and WhatsApp. Use cases span daily planning, calendar management, health and fitness tracking, smart home control, and photo editing — all via text message. The 10-person startup is backed by Spark Capital and General Catalyst, and closed a fresh $10M top-up that values it at $300M post-money.

Apple's Messages for Business requires companies to apply and be approved to reach iPhone users via iMessage. Poke clearing that bar as an AI agent — not a company — signals Apple may be opening a dedicated lane for agentic services inside its messaging stack, just days before WWDC 2026.

## 3️⃣ Congress Tables 269-Page Federal AI Act — States Would Lose Frontier-Model Authority for 3 Years

- **What:** Reps. Jay Obernolte (R-CA) and Lori Trahan (D-MA) unveiled the Great American Artificial Intelligence Act, a 269-page bipartisan discussion draft that would preempt state AI development laws for three years
- **Why it matters:** States like California have led AI regulation at the model-development level; this draft would freeze that authority while Congress builds a federal governance framework, a major power shift in U.S. AI policy
- **Key number:** 269-page draft; 3-year preemption of state laws governing frontier AI model development

The bill organizes around four pillars: frontier model governance, workforce impact monitoring, cybersecurity hardening, and AI R&D investment. Preemption would cover laws "specifically regulating the development" of AI models — states retain authority over how AI systems are deployed and used.

The draft landed to immediate opposition. Public Citizen and union leaders are urging rejection, arguing it strips states of power to protect consumers and workers. The Information Technology Industry Council praised it for codifying the National AI Research Resource and extending the Cybersecurity Information Sharing Act. As a discussion draft, the bill is not yet formally introduced — it's an open invitation for stakeholder feedback before a formal version is filed.
