## 1️⃣ Microsoft's 5-Model Breakup With OpenAI

- **What:** Reuters and The Information (May 29) report Microsoft will unveil five homegrown AI models at Build 2026 (June 2–3, San Francisco): a coding model codenamed Project Polaris, plus MAI-Image-2.5, MAI-Voice-2, MAI-Transcribe-1.5, and a reasoning model.
- **Why it matters:** Claude Code and Cursor have eroded GitHub Copilot's lead; Microsoft is betting its own models will outperform rented OpenAI ones at lower cost while reducing legal and strategic dependency.
- **Key number:** Microsoft shares jumped 3% on the report.

Mustafa Suleiman's internal AI team was freed from contractual constraints after Microsoft renegotiated its OpenAI partnership in April 2026. The breadth of the lineup — spanning code, transcription, reasoning, speech, and image generation — signals a full internal AI stack, not a one-off supplement.

GitHub Copilot has 4.7 million paid subscribers (up 75% year-over-year) but has faced service outages and churn to rivals. Project Polaris is optimized for code understanding and generation; general availability is targeted for August 2026. Azure AI Foundry will also receive major upgrades at Build.

## 2️⃣ Replit + Visa: AI Agents Get Their Own Wallets

- **What:** Visa made a strategic investment in Replit and announced integration of Visa Intelligent Commerce into the Replit platform, enabling AI agents to transact autonomously via Visa's Trusted Agent Protocol (TAP) registry.
- **Why it matters:** Payment rails have been the missing piece in agentic stacks — TAP gives AI agents a verified financial identity and the ability to complete purchases across merchant endpoints without user involvement for every transaction.
- **Key number:** Replit has 30 million developers and $150 million in annualized revenue; enterprise self-serve now available for contracts up to $200,000.

Replit agents can join Visa's TAP registry, marking them as Visa-trusted and allowing them to execute purchases on behalf of users across Visa's merchant network. Developers can access payment building blocks natively inside their Replit workflows, no separate payment integration required.

Beyond the Visa deal, Replit simultaneously launched self-serve enterprise access — companies can buy Replit Enterprise directly for contract values up to $200,000 without engaging a sales team, with enterprise-grade compliance and security controls included.

## 3️⃣ DeepSeek Locks In Its 75% Price Cut Forever

- **What:** DeepSeek made its promotional 75% discount on V4-Pro permanent on May 22, canceling a May 31 expiration date that had been in place since the model's April 24 launch.
- **Why it matters:** V4-Pro is DeepSeek's frontier-tier coding model; locking in the cut forces Western frontier labs to compete on price for the first time at this capability tier.
- **Key number:** V4-Pro input is now $0.435/M tokens — 75% below its original list price of $1.74/M.

The original promotion was set to expire May 31; on May 22, DeepSeek announced it would permanently adjust API pricing to the discounted rate. The result: the cheapest frontier-tier coding model by token cost, priced indefinitely below what OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google charge for comparable output.

DeepSeek is also building agentically: a former Jane Street engineer has been hired to lead a new "AI harness" team building deterministic scaffolding to turn V4 into autonomous, revenue-generating agents — the first clear signal the lab is moving from raw-model R&D into productized agentic offerings.
