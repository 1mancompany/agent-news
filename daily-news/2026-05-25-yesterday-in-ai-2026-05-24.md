## 1️⃣ White House Overrides Pentagon to Secure Anthropic Deal — $9B in AI Chips Approved

- **What:** White House chief of staff Susie Wiles authorized the NSA to keep using Anthropic’s Mythos model and approved a secret $9B chip acquisition, despite the Pentagon having blacklisted Anthropic as a “supply chain risk.”
- **Why it matters:** The Pentagon-Anthropic feud is now fracturing inside the US government itself — the same administration that banned Anthropic from DoD work is quietly writing it a classified contract.
- **Key number:** $9 billion — the emergency chip budget approved for spy agencies to reach frontier AI

A chip shortage forced the issue. Intelligence agencies couldn’t run frontier models on classified networks without a new generation of infrastructure built around Nvidia’s Grace Blackwell superchip, which requires dedicated liquid-cooled data centers. The $9B covers that buildout; Congress must still approve the full amount, but $800M is being reprogrammed immediately for rapid capacity deployment.

Anthropic has restricted Mythos access to roughly 40 organizations, citing its offensive cyber capabilities. The NSA contract includes a carve-out explicitly prohibiting use on Americans’ data, and drops the “any lawful use” language the Defense Department had demanded. Anthropic sued the Trump administration in March to reverse the Pentagon blacklist after refusing to enable mass domestic surveillance and autonomous weapons systems.

## 2️⃣ Google I/O 2026: Gemini 3.5 Flash Becomes the Global Default, Managed Agents API Ships

- **What:** At Google I/O (May 19–20), Google shipped Gemini 3.5 Flash as generally available and the new default model in the Gemini app and Google Search AI Mode worldwide, alongside a Managed Agents API that spins up persistent-state agents in a single call.
- **Why it matters:** With Gemini 3.5 Flash now powering every Search interaction globally, this is the widest single-shot deployment of a new model generation in AI history.
- **Key number:** 4x — Gemini 3.5 Flash’s output token generation speed advantage over competing frontier models

Gemini 3.5 Flash beats Gemini 3.1 Pro on every major coding and agentic benchmark: Terminal-Bench 2.1 (76.2%), GDPval-AA (1,656 Elo), and MCP Atlas (83.6%). At less than half the cost of comparable models, Google positions it as the cost-performance sweet spot for long-horizon agent tasks. Gemini 3.5 Pro is in internal testing and expected next month.

The Managed Agents API is the developer headline: one API call spins up a full agent with persistent state, running in an isolated Google-hosted Linux environment via the Antigravity 2.0 harness. Gemini Spark — Google’s 24/7 personal AI assistant with MCP integrations for Canva, OpenTable, and Instacart — enters beta for Google AI Ultra subscribers starting the week of May 26.

## 3️⃣ GitHub Copilot Abandons Flat Pricing: AI Credits Switch June 1

- **What:** In 7 days, GitHub Copilot drops its flat-rate subscription model and switches all plans to token-based "GitHub AI Credits" billing — 1 credit = $0.01, drawn down per token consumed by agents, chat, and advanced models.
- **Why it matters:** Code completions stay unlimited, but every Copilot agent interaction and premium-model chat now runs on a metered budget — a structural change mirroring Anthropic’s own June 15 programmatic billing split.
- **Key number:** $0.01 per AI Credit — the rate at which agent sessions and advanced-model requests drain monthly budgets

Copilot Pro+ ($39/month) users get 3,900 credits; Business ($19/seat) gets 1,900; Enterprise ($39/seat) gets 3,900. To ease the transition, GitHub is offering promotional bonus credits for June–August: Business seats get 3,000/month instead of 1,900; Enterprise gets 7,000 instead of 3,900.

Code completions and next-edit suggestions — the bread-and-butter of Copilot usage — remain unlimited on all paid plans. The shift reflects GitHub’s recognition that agentic workloads can consume orders of magnitude more tokens than completions, and can’t be sustainably bundled into a flat fee.
