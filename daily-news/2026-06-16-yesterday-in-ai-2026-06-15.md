## 1️⃣ Class Action Filed: Claude Max $200/Month Plan Drained in One 5-Hour Session

- **What:** Karl Kahn filed a federal class-action suit against Anthropic on June 14 in the Northern District of California, alleging the Max 5x ($100/mo) and Max 20x ($200/mo) plans deliver far less usage than advertised.
- **Why it matters:** The suit forces Anthropic to define, in court, what “5x” and “20x” actually mean — a question millions of subscribers have quietly asked as agentic Claude Code workloads spike usage.
- **Key number:** 15% — the share of Kahn’s weekly Max 20x allowance consumed by a single five-hour Claude Code session.

Kahn subscribed to Claude Pro in June 2025, upgraded to Max 5x in January 2026, then paid $200/month for Max 20x in April after ramping up Claude Code use. He alleges usage limits are hard to interpret, shift without notice, and fall well below Anthropic’s marketing language. The complaint seeks class-action status for all US Max subscribers since Anthropic first offered the tier.

The lawsuit is notable for its timing: filed one day before Anthropic’s new programmatic billing structure went live, which separates agentic usage into separate credit pools. That billing change was meant to clarify pricing — instead, it coincides with a lawsuit arguing the original structure was deliberately opaque.

## 2️⃣ Anthropic Meets Trump White House Over Fable 5 Ban — Talks Stall

- **What:** Anthropic senior staff held in-person talks with Trump administration officials in Washington on June 15, attempting to resolve a June 12 directive that suspended all global access to Claude Fable 5 and Mythos 5.
- **Why it matters:** This is the first US export-control action targeting a specific frontier AI model family — a precedent that could affect any lab whose capability crosses an undefined government threshold.
- **Key number:** 0 — breakthroughs from Monday’s in-person talks; both sides remain at an impasse after three days.

The Commerce Department issued its directive after reports that Mythos could be jailbroken to generate cybersecurity exploits. Amazon CEO Andy Jassy reportedly told White House officials that Amazon’s internal researchers had successfully prompted Mythos to produce information useful to hackers bypassing enterprise security systems. Anthropic disabled both models globally for all users — including its own employees — and filed a separate lawsuit against the government to reverse the order.

Monday’s in-person session was part of an ongoing back-and-forth since June 12. No resolution was reached; both sides were described as “still working through it.” Cybersecurity experts publicly questioned the logic, noting that banning one lab’s model rarely prevents capable adversaries from accessing equivalent capabilities elsewhere.

## 3️⃣ Google × Kaggle Launch Vibe-Coding AI Agents Course — 1.5M Alumni Targeted

- **What:** Google and Kaggle opened their second free 5-Day AI Agents Intensive on June 15, adding a “vibe coding” track that lets learners build production agents using natural language rather than traditional code.
- **Why it matters:** The original November 2025 edition enrolled 1.5 million developers — making this one of the few agent-education programs with genuine mass-market reach for developer re-skilling.
- **Key number:** 1.5M+ — learners who enrolled in the original course, now being recruited for the vibe-coding follow-on.

The June 15–19 course covers tool calling, multi-agent coordination, and production deployment. Each day pairs whitepapers, a podcast episode, and hands-on code labs with a live YouTube session featuring Google engineers and Kaggle authors. The capstone produces a working AI agent. The new “vibe coding” track removes the traditional coding prerequisite entirely, using natural language as the primary build interface — a significant lowering of the barrier.

The course runs on Gemini models and Google’s own agent frameworks, co-created with the same team behind the November 2025 edition. Registration is free and open globally.
