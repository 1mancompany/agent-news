## 1️⃣ Anthropic Files Confidential S-1 — $965B Valuation, $47B Run-Rate

- **What:** Anthropic submitted a confidential draft Form S-1 to the SEC on June 1, 2026, beginning the formal process toward a public listing.
- **Why it matters:** At $965B, Anthropic would debut as one of the largest tech IPOs in history and, if markets cooperate, would cross $1 trillion on day one alongside SpaceX and OpenAI in a once-in-a-generation AI listing trifecta.
- **Key number:** $47B — Anthropic's current annualized revenue run-rate, up from near-zero at its 2021 founding.

Anthropic's IPO move follows its February 2026 $65B funding round that pushed its private valuation to $965B. The confidential S-1 track lets the company avoid disclosing financials until 21 days before its roadshow, giving it flexibility to price off private comparables rather than live market sentiment. Analysts at most banks covering the deal put a $1 trillion+ debut as the base case if markets hold.

Revenue has been driven almost entirely by enterprise adoption of Claude for coding and agentic workflows. The three main pillars — the Claude API, Claude Code enterprise subscriptions, and Amazon Bedrock's Claude deployment — each crossed multi-billion-dollar run-rates in the past 12 months. The IPO would mark the first public test of whether AI infrastructure revenue at scale commands software multiples or compute-company multiples.

## 2️⃣ NVIDIA Drops Nemotron 3 Ultra at Computex: Top US Open Weights at 550B Params

- **What:** Jensen Huang announced Nemotron 3 Ultra at Computex 2026 on June 1 — a 550B-parameter MoE open-weights model that scores 48 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, the highest of any US-origin open model.
- **Why it matters:** Nemotron 3 Ultra gives US enterprises a frontier open-weights option without the export-control uncertainty attached to DeepSeek or Qwen, at production-grade inference speed.
- **Key number:** 48 — Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index score, well ahead of Gemma 4 31B (39) and gpt-oss-120b (33).

Nemotron 3 Ultra uses a MoE architecture activating roughly 55B of its 550B parameters per token, delivering over 300 tokens per second on pre-release DeepInfra endpoints. The model is designed specifically for agentic and reasoning workloads. It is the largest member of the Nemotron 3 family, which also ships Nano (multimodal, edge-optimized) and Super (mid-range) variants. Weights become publicly available under a permissive license on June 4.

The announcement lands in the same week Microsoft's Build 2026 kicks off — and with it, a flood of enterprise AI agent tooling. For US-headquartered organizations under ITAR or FedRAMP constraints, having a domestically-controlled 550B open model changes the calculus on self-hosted frontier inference materially.

## 3️⃣ GitHub Copilot Goes Token-Based: AI Credits Replace Flat Seats on June 1

- **What:** GitHub moved all Copilot plans from per-seat pricing to usage-based "AI Credits" billing on June 1, replacing Premium Request Units with a $0.01-per-credit model tied directly to token consumption.
- **Why it matters:** Teams running agentic coding workflows — which burn orders of magnitude more tokens than simple completions — can now face monthly bills that far exceed their nominal plan price.
- **Key number:** $0.01 — the price of one AI Credit; each model call consumes credits proportional to input + output tokens at published API rates.

Every Copilot plan now ships with a monthly credit allotment matching its price: Pro+ ($39/month) includes $39 in credits, Business ($19/user) includes $19 per seat, and Enterprise ($39/user) includes $39. Code completions and Next Edit Suggestions remain unlimited and unmetered. Everything else — Copilot Chat, code review, agent tasks — draws from the pool. Overages are purchasable; user-level budget caps prevent runaway spend but require opt-in setup.

Developer reaction has been strongly negative. Community threads describe the change as "you will get less but pay the same price" — particularly for Pro users who previously received unlimited premium requests. The friction is sharpest for developers who rely on Copilot agents for PR review and multi-file edits, where a single agentic run can consume hundreds of credits. GitHub launched a preview billing dashboard to give admins projected spend visibility before invoices land.
